## Family Law

### Dealing With Division

Before divorce, the law in most places treats husband and wife as one legal unit.

After divorce, the law treats them as separate people again.

That split is not just emotional.

It is legal.

And once it happens, the court has to sort out responsibilities that do not magically disappear just because the relationship did.

Divorce starts with heartbreak, disappointment, and the wreckage of what was supposed to work.

That part is real.

But in court, divorce is also about assignment:

who is responsible for what,
who pays what,
who gets what,
who sees the children when,
and how all of that is enforced after the paperwork is signed.

If children are involved, the stakes get even more serious because now it is not only about two adults trying to untangle a failed marriage.

Now it is also about stability, protection, support, time, and emotional structure for children who did not ask for any of this.

That is why this section matters.

Divorce is painful.

It does not have to be chaotic.

And it definitely does not have to become a lawyer-fed machine designed to drain both sides while solving less than it should.

### What Divorce Actually Means

Divorce legally dissolves the marital unit.

Afterward, both people go forward as separate individuals again.

That does not always mean all obligations end.

In many cases, certain obligations continue for years.

Those may include:

- division of assets
- child support
- child custody
- alimony

If the divorce is handled right, those continuing obligations will be put into enforceable court orders.

And if those orders are violated, the violating party may face contempt, sanctions, and in some cases even jail.

So no, divorce is not just signing a few pages and walking away.

It is restructuring legal duties under the authority of the court.

### The Forms

In most jurisdictions, divorce paperwork is standardized.

The names may shift a little, but the core structure usually looks like this:

- divorce by agreement, no children
- divorce by agreement, with children
- contested divorce, no children
- contested divorce, with children

Underneath all that, there are really only two core situations:

either the parties agree,
or they do not.

That is the divide that changes everything.

## Divorce by Agreement

This is the least destructive version.

Not painless.
Just less destructive.

When the parties actually agree, they can usually fill out the required forms, disclose finances, divide assets and debts, and work out the child-related terms if children are involved.

That means agreeing about:

- the divorce itself
- assets and debts
- who is responsible for which obligations
- who receives which property
- custody
- visitation
- child support

If the court approves the agreement, the court enters an order making those terms enforceable.

And that order should reserve jurisdiction for future enforcement or further orders if justice requires it.

That clause matters.

Without it, you may create avoidable problems later.

In principle, an agreed divorce is simple:

1. the parties agree
2. the court approves
3. the court reserves power to enforce

That is the clean version.

The trouble usually starts later, when one side decides the signed agreement was apparently just a suggestion.

That is when fights begin over:

- debts
- taxes
- transfer of property
- child visitation
- child support
- alimony

So even an agreed divorce needs to be drafted with the future in mind, not just the present relief of getting it done.

## Contested Divorce

This is where lawyers often get rich.

Because contested divorces can be dragged out for years if nobody forces discipline into the process.

And when that happens, both sides bleed.

Money goes.
Time goes.
Peace goes.
Children feel it.

The legal issues in contested divorce are usually the same issues you see after agreed divorce, but now everything is weaponized:

- debt allocation
- parenting time
- custody
- child support
- alimony
- property division

The difference is that instead of agreement, every issue becomes a battlefield.

These battles are usually won or lost through discovery.

Not through dramatic speeches.
Not through emotional outrage alone.
Through discovery.

That means:

- getting financial records
- subpoenaing banks and third parties
- using interrogatories to expose changes in income, living arrangements, or support needs
- taking depositions when necessary
- forcing admissions

Contested divorces become discovery wars.

And if lawyers are involved without discipline, those wars can be stretched far beyond what is necessary while both clients are drained dry.

### Motions to Compel Discovery

When the other side refuses to answer discovery in good faith, do not wait around hoping cooperation suddenly appears.

File the motion.

Immediately.

No soft handling.
No endless patience theater.

If the information you are seeking is reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence, you are entitled to it.

That is the standard.

So when the other side stalls, dodges, or hides behind meaningless objections, move to compel.

Set it for hearing quickly.
Support it with a memorandum if needed.

And if the court orders compliance and the other side still refuses, escalate:

- motion to show cause
- motion for contempt

That is how you force movement in these cases.

Divorce discovery often reveals that the biggest obstacle is not even the spouse.

Sometimes it is the lawyer.

Either way, if facts are being hidden, the answer is not passive frustration.

The answer is motion practice.

## Marital and Non-Marital Property

In most jurisdictions, what you owned before marriage is yours.

That is usually non-marital property.

What is acquired during the marriage is usually marital property.

That means the law generally treats it as belonging to both parties, regardless of whose name appears more often in the story.

That can include:

- homes bought during the marriage
- businesses built during the marriage
- income earned during the marriage
- sometimes even lottery winnings during the marriage

There are exceptions and local variations, but the broad pattern is clear.

If you owned something before the marriage, its pre-marriage value may remain non-marital.

But growth in value during the marriage may be a different issue.

And once you start mixing assets, especially through joint accounts, things get messy fast.

Money placed into a joint account often becomes marital in ways people do not fully understand until it is too late.

So yes, local law matters.

But the general rule is this:

pre-marriage property usually begins as separate,
marriage-era property usually begins as shared,
and commingling creates risk.

## Child Support

Child support is not money for the ex-spouse to do whatever they want with.

It is money for the child.

That should be obvious, but in real life it gets abused constantly.

Most jurisdictions calculate support using some formula tied to income, assets, or both.

The exact chart or statute changes by state, but the principle stays the same: the non-custodial parent contributes financially to the child's needs.

And those needs belong to the child.

Not the ex-spouse's nightlife.
Not the ex-spouse's personal shopping.
Not the ex-spouse's unrelated comfort.

If you are paying support, you have every right to care where it is going.

And if you have reason to believe it is not being used for the child, that is not something you quietly swallow forever.

That is something you investigate through discovery and raise with the court.

Child support should be paid in a way that can be proven.

That means:

- by check
- by traceable transfer
- by some method that leaves evidence

Not cash unless there is a signed receipt with real detail.

And do not assume groceries, clothes, bikes, or random purchases count as support just because you spent money.

Courts may treat those as gifts.

Child support is money support.

If you want credit, create proof.

### Child Custody

Every parent of a minor child has a fundamental interest in time, relationship, and connection with that child.

The degree of custody or visitation can change based on safety, stability, addiction, violence, crime, or other serious concerns.

But absent those things, the court is dealing with human bonds that matter deeply.

And once the child reaches adulthood, the court's control over that relationship changes because the child becomes free to decide for themselves.

### UCCJEA

Most states have adopted the Uniform Child Custody Jurisdiction and Enforcement Act.

Know it.

Learn how your state applies it.

Memorize the parts that matter if custody is in dispute across state lines or if enforcement becomes necessary.

This is not trivia in family law.

It is foundational.

The exact wording may shift from state to state, but the core rule does not: this statute helps determine where custody disputes belong, how existing orders are enforced, and when a court has authority to step in.

Do not treat that lightly.

Get your state's version.
Read it.
Understand it.
Cite it.

Then go find the appellate opinions that explain how it is applied in real cases, because that is where the abstract language gets turned into courtroom reality.

### The Child's Best Interest

This is the center of the whole custody fight.

Not your convenience.
Not your ex's irritation.
Not which adult feels more put out.

The standard is what is in the best interest of the child.

That is the rule.

If a parent stands up in court and complains mainly about inconvenience, cost, burden, or frustration, that argument is weak unless those things genuinely affect the child's welfare.

Make your arguments about the child:

- safety
- health
- emotional stability
- education
- living environment
- long-term welfare

That is what carries weight.

### Custody Changes

Custody orders are not permanently frozen.

If circumstances materially change, the court can revisit them.

That matters because bad orders are not always final in a practical sense.

If the primary custodial parent becomes unsafe, unstable, addicted, incarcerated, or unable to maintain a healthy environment, or if the other parent becomes substantially better positioned to provide what the child needs, the issue can come back before the court.

But do not walk in with just opinion.

Walk in with evidence:

- certified records
- reports
- school documentation
- inspection records
- admissible testimony

The court should act on significant change, not vague dissatisfaction.

## Alimony

Alimony law differs from state to state, but the underlying point is consistent: where one spouse leaves the marriage in a much stronger financial position and the other cannot reasonably maintain an acceptable standard of support without help, the court may order assistance.

That assistance may be:

- temporary
- lump-sum
- periodic
- long-term in rare or severe cases

The exact result depends on the statutes, the length of the marriage, the earning abilities of the parties, and whether the receiving spouse can realistically become self-supporting.

Many states now lean toward temporary support instead of permanent support, but disability or other severe limitations can change that analysis.

And yes, remarriage generally ends ongoing alimony.

Read your state's statutes.
Read the appellate decisions interpreting them.
That is the only way to argue alimony with any real force.

## Family Court Rules

Family court does not operate in some lawless emotional fog.

There may be separate family-court rules, but those rules often incorporate major parts of the evidence rules and civil procedure rules.

So know:

- the family-court rules
- the rules of evidence
- the rules of civil procedure

If a rule limits who may attend hearings, enforce it.

If a rule restricts unreliable testimony from very young children, know it and use it.

And if a judge allows procedure or evidence that crosses the line, make your record so the issue is preserved.

Rules are tools.

Use them.

## Child Custody

### Who Should Have Your Children?

If someone is threatening your relationship with your children, identify exactly who that person or entity is and exactly what they are claiming.

Do not fight fog.

Find the source.

It may be:

- an ex-spouse
- a family member
- a doctor
- a neighbor
- a state agency
- a foster system
- some other interfering outsider

The right to raise your own children is one of the most serious rights a person has.

If someone is trying to fracture that bond, you need clarity, law, evidence, and pressure.

### Did Someone Lie About You?

This is one of the first questions to answer.

False accusations in custody-related settings often involve claims of:

- drug abuse
- medical neglect
- dangerous visitors
- criminal history
- emotional abuse
- physical abuse
- sexual abuse
- school neglect
- failure to feed or clothe the child

Sometimes those allegations are real.
Sometimes they are weaponized lies.

When they are false, they can still do immense damage before the truth catches up.

That is why you need to find out:

- who said it
- what they said
- who heard it
- what basis, if any, they had
- how it affected the custody situation

### If They Did Not Lie, Were They Negligent?

Not every custody disaster is driven by a malicious lie.

Sometimes it comes from laziness, arrogance, incompetence, or reckless indifference by agency workers or other decision-makers.

That matters too.

If the harm came from negligence rather than deliberate falsehood, the legal theory should match that reality.

Do not force every case into the wrong cause of action.

Look at the facts.
Then choose the right claim.

And remember: a separate damages lawsuit can give you discovery power that a direct agency dispute may not.

### What Are Parental Rights?

Parental rights include the right to care for your children, make decisions for them, stand for them in court, and guide their upbringing.

But those rights are tied to responsibility.

The law does not protect abuse, abandonment, dangerous conduct, or behavior that destroys a child's welfare.

Rights and duty live together.

That is the truth of parenthood under the law.

### Who Should Have Priority?

Historically, courts often favored mothers, especially in the earliest years.

Modern law talks more about equality, but custody still turns on actual facts, actual care, and actual ability to meet the child's needs.

If you are the father, prove you are the better custodial choice.

If you are the mother, do not assume biology alone wins the day.

Prove that you can protect, nurture, stabilize, and provide.

And if someone outside the parental relationship is trying to interfere without lawful grounds, fight that challenge hard.

### What Matters Most

One thing:

the best interests of the child.

That is the standard.

The court is supposed to care about:

- physical health
- emotional health
- education
- safety
- quality of life

So make your case through those facts.

Show stable housing.
Show financial support.
Show consistent care.
Show that what you are asking for benefits the child, not just your own comfort.

### What Does the Law Say?

Research it.

What you believe does not control the courtroom.
What random people online say definitely does not.

Read the statutes.
Read the cases.
Read the rules.

Your children are not property.

They are human beings with rights, and the law treats parenthood as an obligation to protect those rights.

That is why legal authority matters.

### What Is the UCCJEA?

Almost every state has some version of the Uniform Child Custody Jurisdiction and Enforcement Act.

Start there when jurisdiction or enforcement is in dispute.

Find your state's official version.
Back it up with controlling appellate law.
Ignore the clutter that masquerades as legal guidance.

Only then are you prepared to fight with something more than emotion.

### The Fight

If you are taking on a child-services agency or any party threatening your custody rights, treat it like litigation.

Because that is exactly what it is.

You need:

- pleadings
- discovery
- motions

And you need actual causes of action.

Do not file loose slogans about constitutional rights and call that a case.

If someone lied, slander or a related claim may be part of the answer.
If someone acted carelessly, negligence may fit better.
If you do not know who did the damage, discovery is how you find them.

### Sample Emergency Complaint

IN THE FOURTH JUDICIAL CIRCUIT COURT  
IN AND FOR REDWOOD COUNTY, CALIFORNIA  
FAMILY DIVISION  
Case No. 12345

ELENA CARTER,  
Plaintiff,

v.

REDWOOD COUNTY DIVISION OF CHILD SERVICES and  
JOHN DOE and JANE DOE, unknown persons,  
Defendants.

________________________/

EMERGENCY COMPLAINT FOR SLANDER AND CHILD CUSTODY

Plaintiff Elena Carter sues Redwood County Division of Child Services and John Doe and Jane Doe, unknown persons, for slander and custody regarding her nine-year-old daughter, Lila Carter, and states:

JURISDICTIONAL ALLEGATIONS

1. This is an action for money damages in excess of $50,000.  
2. This action is brought to obtain lawful custody of a child and to recover money damages for slander.  
3. All acts material to these proceedings took place in Redwood County, California.  
4. This Court has jurisdiction.

FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

5. Unknown persons knowingly published false allegations of material fact to the County concerning Plaintiff's ability to properly and adequately care for her child, Lila Carter.  
6. Through discovery, Plaintiff will identify the unknown person or persons, the exact allegations made, the falsity of those allegations or the reckless lack of evidence supporting them, how and to whom they were published, and the impact they had on Plaintiff's custody rights.  
7. But for the false and baseless allegations published to the County, Plaintiff would now have lawful custody of her child.  
8. Justice requires that no action be taken regarding Plaintiff's custody rights based on allegations that are untrue or unsupported by admissible direct evidence.  
9. County has denied Plaintiff knowledge of the identity of the speaker or speakers and the content of the allegations despite Plaintiff's repeated reasonable requests.  
10. Due process and the interests of justice require immediate disclosure of every person who supplied information to the County regarding Plaintiff's fitness to keep custody of her child.  
11. Time is of the essence.

WHEREFORE, Plaintiff moves this Honorable Court to enter an Emergency Order requiring County to identify all persons who provided information regarding Plaintiff's fitness to enjoy custody of Lila Carter and, upon Plaintiff's showing by admissible evidence that such information was false and possibly known to be false when given, to order the immediate return of the child to Plaintiff's custody and award money damages for slander against the responsible party or parties, together with such other and further relief as this Court deems just and reasonable under the circumstances.

I CERTIFY that a copy of the foregoing was provided to Redwood County's attorney, Martin Hale, Esq., 52 Harbor View Drive, Pacific Grove, California, this 6th day of May 2022.

____________________________  
Elena Carter, Plaintiff

This sample is a template, not a shortcut.

Depending on the facts, you may need multiple causes of action, different procedural vehicles, or a different court posture entirely.

Use the structure.
Then tailor it to reality.

### How Soon Should You File?

Do not file before you are ready.

That does not mean delay forever.

It means know the law, know the rules, know the proof you need, and prepare for a fight before you pull the trigger.

### What Evidence Do You Need?

Start with the essentials:

- who lied
- what was said
- who heard it
- how it harmed your custody position
- what motive drove it

### How Do You Get the Evidence?

Use the five discovery tools in a disciplined order:

1. requests for admissions
2. requests for production
3. interrogatories
4. depositions
5. subpoenas

### Conclusion

Nothing cuts like losing a child.

If that loss came from reckless agency conduct or outright lies, there may be a legal remedy.

And if you are a good parent, the law should be used boldly to protect your place in your children's lives and to force accountability on the people who damaged that bond.

## Discovery

### Tools to Get the Evidence

Pleadings tell the court what must be proved.

Discovery is how you get the proof.

You have five core tools:

1. requests for admissions
2. requests for production
3. interrogatories
4. depositions
5. subpoenas and other court orders

That is it.

Learn them, and use them hard.

If your case is winnable, good discovery should put real pressure on the other side before trial.

And yes, discovery can reach material that is not itself admissible so long as it is reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence.

Never forget that.

### What Can Be Discovered?

Anything reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence.

That can be a contract, a deed, a physical object, a letter that points you to a witness, or some seemingly ordinary item that helps prove an important fact.

If it gets you closer to usable proof, it may be discoverable.

Do not let anyone gaslight you out of that rule.

If someone objects that the material is not admissible, remind the court that admissibility is not the discovery standard.

The standard is whether the material is reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence.

Know the rule.
Cite the rule.
Use the rule.

### Stay Tied to the Pleadings

There is no point chasing facts that do not help prove or disprove an essential fact alleged in the pleadings.

Make a list of those essential facts.

That list is your discovery map.

For each fact, identify which discovery tool is most likely to get you where you need to go.

That keeps you focused and keeps the other side from burying the case in distractions.

### Written Discovery Comes First

Discovery comes in two forms:

- written
- spoken

Written discovery includes:

- pleadings used for discovery purposes
- requests for admissions
- requests for production
- interrogatories
- document subpoenas

Spoken discovery includes:

- depositions
- live testimony at hearings
- live testimony at trial

Use written discovery first.

It lets you word things carefully, box your opponent in, and learn the terrain before you spend time and money on live testimony.

### Requests for Admissions

This is one of the strongest tools in the whole process.

It lets you force the other side to admit or deny specific facts.

If they do not respond properly, the court may treat the matters as admitted.

That is leverage.

Each request should be a clear separately numbered statement.

Do not blur issues together.

Sample rewritten form:

IN THE THIRTIETH JUDICIAL CIRCUIT COURT  
IN AND FOR REDWOOD COUNTY, FLORIDA  
Case No. 2012-123  
Judge Holloway

MAYA MERCER,  
Plaintiff,

v.

ADRIAN COLE,  
Defendant.

____________________/

REQUEST FOR ADMISSIONS

Plaintiff Maya Mercer requests Defendant Adrian Cole to admit the truth of the following statements of fact:

1. You were employed by Plaintiff to deliver produce.  
2. You were allowed to use Plaintiff's produce delivery truck.  
3. On May 17, 2012, you signed a document in the presence of Plaintiff, who also signed the document in your presence.  
4. You received $3,000 from Plaintiff on May 17, 2012.

RESPECTFULLY SUBMITTED this ___ day of ____________ 2012.

____________________________  
Maya Mercer, Plaintiff  
[Certificate of Service]

You can also use admissions to establish authenticity or content of attached exhibits.

Example:

REQUEST FOR ADMISSIONS

Plaintiff Maya Mercer requests Defendant Adrian Cole to admit the truth of the following statements of fact:

1. The document attached as Exhibit A accurately states Rule 1.370 of the Florida Rules of Civil Procedure.  
2. The document attached as Exhibit B accurately states the full contents of a written contract dated May 17, 2012 and signed by you.

RESPECTFULLY SUBMITTED this ___ day of ____________ 2016.

____________________________  
Maya Mercer, Plaintiff  
[Certificate of Service]

Admissions that land in the court file can be used without dragging in extra proof to establish what was already admitted.

If the other side lies, use the rest of discovery to prove it.

Credibility collapses fast once dishonesty is exposed.

### Requests for Production

If the item is reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence, push for it.

That may include:

- financial records
- medical records
- payroll documents
- contracts
- licenses
- corporate records
- physical objects

Be specific.
Use time limits when needed.
Do not ask for everything under the sun if a narrower request will pin the issue down more effectively.

Sample rewritten form:

IN THE THIRTIETH JUDICIAL CIRCUIT COURT  
IN AND FOR REDWOOD COUNTY, FLORIDA  
Case No. 2012-123  
Judge Holloway

MAYA MERCER,  
Plaintiff,

v.

ADRIAN COLE,  
Defendant.

____________________/

REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION

Plaintiff Maya Mercer requests Defendant Adrian Cole to produce for inspection and copying the originals of the following documents and things at Plaintiff's offices or such other place as the parties may agree:

1. All corporate records of Produce Delivery Corporation identifying officers and directors actively engaged in the business from May 17, 2012 to the present.  
2. All records of money or other consideration received by you for sale or delivery of produce from May 17, 2012 to the present, including but not limited to invoices and bank statements.

RESPECTFULLY SUBMITTED this ___ day of ____________ 2012.

____________________________  
Maya Mercer, Plaintiff  
[Certificate of Service]

### Interrogatories

Interrogatories are written questions the other side must answer in writing and under oath.

They are simple.
They are powerful.

Do not waste them on trivia.

Use them for facts that matter and for information you cannot get more efficiently another way.

Make sure the actual party signs the answers under oath.

Save some for later if your rules limit how many you can serve.

Sample rewritten form:

IN THE THIRTIETH JUDICIAL CIRCUIT COURT  
IN AND FOR REDWOOD COUNTY, FLORIDA  
Case No. 2012-123  
Judge Holloway

MAYA MERCER,  
Plaintiff,

v.

ADRIAN COLE,  
Defendant.

____________________/

FIRST SET OF INTERROGATORIES

Plaintiff Maya Mercer propounds the following interrogatories to Defendant Adrian Cole:

1. List the names, addresses, and telephone numbers, if known, of all customers to whom you sold or delivered produce from May 17, 2012 to the present, and state the gross revenues received from each such customer.  
2. List the names, addresses, and telephone numbers, if known, of all persons having any knowledge of your produce sales from May 17, 2012 to the present.  
3. List the names, addresses, and telephone numbers, if known, of all persons you intend to call as witnesses at trial in your defense.  
4. List the names, addresses, and telephone numbers, if known, of all persons holding shares in Produce Delivery Corporation at any time from May 17, 2012 to the present.

RESPECTFULLY SUBMITTED this ___ day of ____________ 2012.

____________________________  
Maya Mercer, Plaintiff  
[Certificate of Service]

Specific dates tighten the request and reduce room for games.

That is not a technicality.

That is leverage.

### Depositions

Depositions let you put witnesses and parties under oath before trial and question them directly.

Done well, they lock stories down.
Done too early, they waste money and create avoidable mess.

That is why written discovery should usually come first.

By the time you take a deposition, you should already know enough to spot contradictions immediately.

Depositions are recorded.
Transcripts can be used later.
Prior sworn testimony can be used to expose trial lies.

Do not chain yourself to a rigid written script.

Know the essential facts you need.
Let those facts guide your questioning.

If the other side turns the deposition into obstruction or bullying, protect the record and, if necessary, suspend the deposition and move for a court order controlling it.

Sample rewritten notice:

IN THE THIRTIETH JUDICIAL CIRCUIT COURT  
IN AND FOR REDWOOD COUNTY, FLORIDA  
Case No. 2012-123  
Judge Holloway

MAYA MERCER,  
Plaintiff,

v.

ADRIAN COLE,  
Defendant.

____________________/

NOTICE OF TAKING DEPOSITION

YOU ARE HEREBY NOTIFIED that the undersigned will take the deposition of Maya Mercer at the offices of Meridian Deposition Services, 515 North Flagler Drive, West Palm Beach, Florida 33401, at 9:30 a.m. on June 12, 2012.

This deposition is for discovery and for use at hearings and at trial.

TIME RESERVED is six (6) hours.

GOVERN YOURSELVES ACCORDINGLY.

____________________________  
Adrian Cole, Defendant  
[Certificate of Service]

### Subpoenas

Subpoenas are court commands to non-parties.

They can require someone to:

- appear at trial
- appear at a hearing
- appear for deposition
- bring documents or objects
- allow inspection of property

Their reach is still tied to the scope of discovery, which means the request must be reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence.

That gives you wide room to use them strategically.

One example from the source makes the point clearly: a judge ordered the sheriff to send a deputy to help clear customers and employees out of a pharmacy, the doors were padlocked, and the evidence needed to win the case was secured.

That is the force behind a real court order.

Court orders are not suggestions.

They are commands.

If people ignore them, judges can respond with contempt, jail, or other enforcement.

That is why subpoenas matter.

They can be used to reach bank records, non-party witnesses, institutional records, and all kinds of information that would otherwise stay out of your hands.

The power sits in the command itself:

`YOU ARE COMMANDED`

Use the local rules to learn how subpoenas are issued and served where you are, then use them with precision.

### Filing Your Evidence

Getting evidence is not enough.

You also need to make sure the evidence makes its way into the court file when it needs to.

That matters because if a key deposition transcript, production response, or sworn interrogatory answer never gets filed, an appellate court may never see it if you have to appeal.

Once the case is closed, it may be too late to fix that.

One way to get important material into the record is through a Notice of Filing.

Sample rewritten form:

IN THE THIRTIETH JUDICIAL CIRCUIT COURT  
IN AND FOR REDWOOD COUNTY, FLORIDA  
Case No. 2012-123  
Judge Holloway

MAYA MERCER,  
Plaintiff,

v.

ADRIAN COLE,  
Defendant.

____________________/

NOTICE OF FILING

Plaintiff Maya Mercer hereby gives notice of filing the following documents attached as exhibits numbered as follows:

1. Copy of transcript of the February 12, 2020 deposition of Adrian Cole.  
2. Copy of documents provided to Plaintiff in Defendant's response to Plaintiff's First Request for Production.  
3. Copy of Defendant's sworn written response to Plaintiff's First Set of Interrogatories.

____________________________  
Maya Mercer, Plaintiff

File copies, not originals.

If authenticity later becomes an issue, that can be addressed with the originals at the proper time.

Do not mail out your originals.

And as always, check the local rules because filing practices can vary.

### Discovery Conclusion

Clever discovery work wins cases.

Some discovery begins right in the pleadings.

Then you have the three written discovery tools.

Then, when needed, you use depositions, subpoenas, and court orders to close the remaining gaps and force the truth into the record.

Discovery proves or disproves the allegations in the pleadings.

That is the whole point.

### Quiz on Discovery

Read carefully, go back over the material until it is solid, and do not walk into court half-prepared.
