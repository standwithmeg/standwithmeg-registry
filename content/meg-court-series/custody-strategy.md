# Custody Strategy for Pro Se Parents

Every parent of a minor child has a fundamental interest in time, relationship, and connection with that child. The degree of custody or visitation can change based on safety, stability, addiction, violence, or crime — but absent those things, the court is dealing with human bonds that matter deeply.

## Legal vs Physical Custody

Legal custody = decision-making authority over education, medical care, religion.
Physical custody = where the child lives.

Both can be sole or joint. Courts frequently grant joint legal custody even when one parent has primary physical custody. If you share legal custody and the other parent is making major decisions without you, that is a violation you can bring back to court.

## The Best Interest Standard

Almost every state uses some version of "best interests of the child." The specific factors vary by state but typically include:

- stability of each parent's home
- each parent's relationship with the child
- the child's adjustment to home, school, and community
- each parent's willingness to support the other parent's relationship with the child
- history of domestic violence or substance abuse
- the child's own wishes (weight varies by age and state)

That last factor — willingness to support the relationship — is one courts take seriously. A parent who blocks calls, sabotages visits, or alienates the child from the other parent is hurting their own case.

## Modifying a Custody Order

Custody orders are not permanent. They can be modified when there is a material change in circumstances since the last order was entered.

What qualifies as material change:
- one parent relocating
- significant change in a parent's work schedule
- child's needs changing (school, medical, age)
- evidence of abuse, neglect, or endangerment
- the child expressing a strong preference (at older ages)

To modify: file a motion to modify custody, allege the specific change in circumstances, and request the new arrangement. Discovery is often essential — if the other parent's living situation has changed, use interrogatories and requests for production to document it.

## UCCJEA — Jurisdiction Matters

Most states have adopted the Uniform Child Custody Jurisdiction and Enforcement Act. If your case crosses state lines — either parent has moved — jurisdiction rules determine which court has authority.

The home state (where the child lived for the last 6 months) generally has jurisdiction. If the other parent filed in a different state after you moved, jurisdiction may be contested. Raise this early — a court without jurisdiction cannot enter enforceable orders.

## Discovery in Custody Cases

Custody battles are often won through discovery, not dramatic testimony.

Use interrogatories to ask:
- Current address and all addresses in the past 2 years
- Names of all persons living in the household
- Employment and schedule information
- Any criminal charges, DCF contacts, or protective orders

Use requests for production to get:
- School records and attendance
- Medical records
- Texts or communications showing parenting conduct

If the other side stonewalls, file a motion to compel immediately. Do not wait.
