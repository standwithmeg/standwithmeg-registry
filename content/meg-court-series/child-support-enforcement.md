# Child Support: Paying, Enforcing, and Protecting Yourself

Child support is money for the child. Not for the ex-spouse's personal expenses. The child is the beneficiary — and that framing matters when you need to go back to court over how it is being used or whether it is being paid.

## How Support Is Calculated

Most states use an income-based formula. Inputs typically include:
- gross income of both parents
- number of children
- parenting time split
- health insurance costs
- childcare costs

The exact formula is state-specific. Look up your state's child support guidelines — they are public and most states have an online calculator. Knowing the formula tells you what to expect and whether an existing order is accurate.

## Pay in a Way You Can Prove

If you are paying support, every payment must be traceable:
- Check with the payee's name in the memo line
- Bank transfer with a clear description
- Through the state's official payment registry (required in many states)

Do not pay cash without a signed, dated receipt that specifies the amount and what it covers. Do not assume groceries, clothing, or random purchases count as support. Courts typically treat those as gifts. If you want credit for them, create proof at the time.

Paying through the state registry is often required and always protects you — the state keeps the record. If you pay directly, keep every receipt, screenshot, and bank statement.

## Enforcing Support When It Is Not Being Paid

If the other parent is not paying:

**Contempt of Court** — the most direct tool. File a motion for contempt, document the missed payments, and ask the court to order payment plus sanctions. Courts can impose fines, revoke licenses, or order jail time for willful nonpayment.

**Income Withholding Order** — in most states, support can be automatically withheld from the paying parent's paycheck. If this is not already in your order, request it.

**State Child Support Enforcement** — every state has a child support enforcement agency (often called IV-D services). They can track income, issue income withholding orders, intercept tax refunds, and suspend licenses. This service is available to any custodial parent. File with your state agency if the other parent has stopped paying.

## Modifying Support

Support can be modified when there is a substantial change in circumstances — either parent's income changes significantly, parenting time changes, or the child's needs change significantly.

To modify: file a motion to modify child support with documentation of the change. Attach income evidence (pay stubs, tax returns) and request a hearing. Do not just stop paying or start paying less — a court order controls until a court changes it.

## When You Believe Support Is Being Misused

You cannot demand receipts for how support money is spent under most state laws — courts generally do not micromanage this. However, if the child is not receiving basic care (food, clothing, housing, medical care) while support is being paid, that is a separate issue: it may support a custody modification argument based on neglect or instability.

Document what you observe. Bring it as a factor in a custody modification, not as a direct demand for accounting.
