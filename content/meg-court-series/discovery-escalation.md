# Discovery: How to Force the Truth Out

Discovery is how contested cases get won. Not through dramatic speeches. Not through emotional outrage alone. Through discovery — getting facts into the record that the other side cannot deny.

## The Four Core Discovery Tools

**Interrogatories** — written questions the other party must answer under oath. Use these to establish facts, expose changes in income or living arrangements, and get admissions. Keep questions specific. Vague interrogatories get vague answers.

**Requests for Production** — demands for documents, records, photos, texts, communications. If you need bank records, medical records, texts, or reports — this is how you get them. Be specific about what you want and the time range.

**Subpoenas** — court-issued demands sent directly to third parties (banks, employers, agencies, therapists). If the other side won't produce records voluntarily, subpoena the source directly.

**Depositions** — live sworn testimony taken outside of court, before trial. Expensive but powerful. Forces witnesses to commit to their story on the record. Inconsistencies in deposition testimony can be used at trial.

## The Escalation Ladder

When the other side refuses to cooperate with discovery, here is the sequence:

1. **Send a good faith letter** — document that you tried to resolve it informally. Courts expect this before you file.
2. **File a Motion to Compel** — ask the court to order compliance. Include the original request, the inadequate response, and your legal argument that the information is discoverable. Set it for a hearing quickly.
3. **Motion to Show Cause / Contempt** — if they get an order to comply and still refuse, now you are seeking sanctions. Courts take contempt seriously.
4. **Motion for Sanctions** — courts can strike pleadings, award attorney fees, or even enter default judgment against a party who willfully refuses discovery.

Do not wait around hoping cooperation suddenly appears. If the information is reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence, you are entitled to it. File the motion.

## What "Reasonably Calculated" Means

The standard for discovery relevance is broader than trial relevance. You can obtain information that might lead to admissible evidence, even if the information itself would not be admitted at trial. This is intentionally broad. Use it.

If the other side objects that something is not relevant, push back. Ask them to identify which specific element of their case or yours makes the information irrelevant. Vague objections to discovery are often not valid.

## Protecting Yourself in Discovery

If you receive a discovery request you believe is improper — harassing, overbroad, or seeking privileged information — you have options:

- Object specifically and in writing within the deadline
- File a Motion for Protective Order if the requests are genuinely abusive
- Do not just ignore it — failing to respond to discovery is one of the fastest ways to lose ground in a case

Answer what you can. Object to what you cannot. Document everything.
