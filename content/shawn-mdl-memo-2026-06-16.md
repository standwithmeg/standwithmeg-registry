- **Key takeaway:** The data shows dense, repeating networks of the *same judges, attorneys, GALs, and CPS workers* appearing across dozens of unrelated families. That pattern is the strongest litigation hook — but a traditional MDL typically needs a common defendant. The better near-term path may be a **DOJ fraud referral** or **state-by-state civil rights / RICO pattern** litigation.

## 1. Repeat Actors / Institutional Defendants
The strongest pattern is not one “Purdue.” It is the *same individuals* named repeatedly across families in the same court system. Repeated names suggest either institutional roles (e.g., court-appointed GAL office, CPS agency, evaluator panel) or a small court pool that families cannot escape.

## 5. Strategic Takeaways for Shawn
1. **No single “Purdue” yet, but strong pattern defendants exist.** Look at the top public actors and the courts/counties where they operate. The same GAL office, evaluator panel, or CPS contractor serving one judge’s docket can function as a de facto institutional defendant.
2. **The fraud angle has data support.** GAL/evaluator/reunification fees appear across thousands of families. If those providers billed Medicaid or were court-ordered by the same judges, that is a traceable pattern.
3. **Geographic clustering is extreme.** A handful of states and counties dominate the dataset. That makes state AG complaints, FBI field-office referrals, and targeted civil suits practical.
4. **The DOJ path is the cleanest first step.** A joint petition to the National Fraud Enforcement Division, backed by this aggregate data plus curated case packets, lets the government identify the central bad actor instead of you guessing.
5. **If an MDL is ever viable, the hook would likely be a *product* or *service* common across districts** — for example, a specific custody-evaluation methodology, a specific supervised-visitation vendor chain, or a specific court-appointed guardian program that operates in multiple federal districts.

## 6. Recommended Next Deliverables
1. **Narrow “case packet” cohort:** Pick the top 5 public actors and pull anonymized, redacted case packets for families who named them. Shawn can review for wire-fraud elements.
2. **Provider billing analysis:** For the top 10 counties, identify repeat GAL/evaluator/therapist names and cross-check against state licensing boards and Medicaid billing data (where publicly available).
3. **Federal-district mapping:** Map the top counties to federal judicial districts. An MDL only applies to federal cases in multiple districts; knowing the district footprint is required.
4. **Civil-rights pattern memo:** Analyze whether the same due-process violations appear in the same court systems, which could support a § 1983 / pattern-or-practice case even without a traditional MDL.
